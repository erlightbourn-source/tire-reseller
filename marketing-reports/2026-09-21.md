## NEW FINDING 1 — the message-composer consent checkbox resets per listing, adding
## repeated friction to the highest-intent activation action

**Stage:** Activation → Conversion (buyer → seller contact)
**What & why:** `components/MessageSeller.js:12` (`const [ack, setAck] = useState(false)`)
means the new "I understand these tires are sold as-is... at my own risk" checkbox
(shipped alongside the hardened liability terms) starts unchecked on every listing-detail
page load, and the **Send message** button stays disabled until it's re-checked
(`disabled={busy || !ack}`, line 68). A buyer comparing 3-4 listings — completely normal
browse behavior on a marketplace, and the exact moment TireTrader most wants a message
sent — has to notice and re-tick a small checkbox on every single listing before they can
send, with no memory of having already agreed on the last one two minutes ago.
Compare to the signup checkbox this was clearly modeled on (`AuthForm.js`): that one is a
**one-time** gate, asked once per account, which is the right shape for a real assent step.
Re-asking it per-listing turns a one-time legal acknowledgment into a recurring
speed-bump on the core conversion action, which is a different (and worse) UX than what was
presumably intended.
**Where:** `components/MessageSeller.js:12` (state), `:64` (checkbox), `:69` (send button).
**Options** (not a monetization/schema call — pure client-state, data-engineer-safe):
  1. **Persist the ack for the session** (e.g. `sessionStorage`/a cookie flag set once
     checked) so a buyer who already agreed once this session doesn't re-see friction on
     the next listing. Keeps the real legal assent (still required at least once) without
     penalizing normal multi-listing browsing.
  2. **Persist per-account** (a lightweight `User` field or timestamp, checked once ever)
     if the legal intent is "acknowledged at least once as an account holder," mirroring
     the signup Terms checkbox's one-time model exactly.
  Recommend option 1 as the minimal-risk fix; option 2 needs a schema field so it's a
  human/design call, not something to auto-implement here.
**Effort:** S (session-persisted) / M (account-persisted, needs schema). **Confidence:**
high that the current per-listing reset is unintentional friction, not a deliberate choice —
nothing in the commit history or BACKLOG suggests "re-ask every time" was the goal.

## NEW FINDING 2 — the arbitration/class-action-waiver line now shows on every login,
## not just once

**Stage:** Trust (but a possible conversion cost on returning users)
**What & why:** `components/AuthForm.js:218-225` — the new post-signup hardened terms
(BACKLOG #11, attorney-reviewed, Evan's deliberate call to maximize liability protection)
added a line below the **Log in** button, on every login page render, restating "including
a binding arbitration & class-action waiver." The signup version of this (checkbox, checked
once at account creation) is the right shape — a real assent gate. The login-page version
is not a gate at all (it's unconditional static text, not tied to any action or checkbox),
so it doesn't add legal protection beyond what was already captured at signup — it only adds
visual weight to a legal/adversarial phrase on a screen a returning user might see dozens of
times. For a pre-launch marketplace trying to look approachable to its first 25 sellers, a
returning user re-reading "class-action waiver" on every login is a small but real trust-tone
cost with no matching legal benefit (the binding agreement already happened at signup).
**Where:** `components/AuthForm.js:218-225`.
**Recommendation:** since the login-page text isn't a consent gate, consider dropping it (the
signup-time checkbox is where the real assent lives) or shortening it to a plain "By
continuing, you agree to our Terms and Privacy Policy" without restating the arbitration
clause specifically — the full clause is still one click away via the Terms link either way.
**This is a legal-copy call, not a pure UX call** — flagging for Evan/counsel to confirm
before any change, since BACKLOG #11 shows this language went through two attorney reviews
already and there may be a reason (e.g. re-affirmation on every session) this repo's authors
don't have visibility into.
**Effort:** S (copy-only, if approved). **Confidence:** medium — real observation, but the
legal rationale might outweigh the UX cost; needs a human call either way.

## ONE POSITIVE OBSERVATION — the required-State listing flow (PR #41) is clean

**Stage:** Seller conversion / Discovery
**What:** `components/ListingForm.js`'s new required State dropdown (alphabetized by name,
separate from the free-text City field) is a good pattern: it fixes the real "no tires in
Florida" bug this shipped alongside, doesn't add a text-entry burden (dropdown, not typing),
and the helper copy ("Buyers filter by state — required so your listing shows up") correctly
explains *why* the field is required instead of just marking it mandatory — that's the right
way to add required-field friction without it feeling arbitrary. `app/browse/page.js`
confirms the state filter is fully wired end-to-end (URL param, empty-state copy, canonical
tag). No action needed — noting it as the reference shape for the next required-field
addition.
