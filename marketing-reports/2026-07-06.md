# TireTrader — Growth & UX Funnel Review
**Advisory memo · read-only analysis · 2026-07-06**

Scope: homepage, browse, listing detail, seller flows (sell-tires / subscribe / pro / sell), auth, seller profiles, SEO landing pages (sizes/tires), `lib/content.js`, `lib/pricing.js`, `lib/safety.js`, and the frozen `docs/index.html`. Every item below cites the screen + file it came from. Ranked by leverage across the funnel, launch-relevant trust/conversion items weighted up.

One framing note the whole memo builds on (from Ada's 2026-07-05 market intel): the defensible wedge in used tires is **"is this tire safe, and will it fit?"** — verified condition, a returns/guarantee mechanism, and a clean hand-off. Incumbents win on breadth; TireTrader has to win on trust. Roughly a third of tire shoppers start online but only ~13% buy online, so the funnel's job at launch is to close the research-online / buy-offline gap with trust signals, not to out-SKU SimpleTire.

---

## The single biggest theme
The product **already collects and displays the right trust primitives** — tread depth, DOT year with an aging warning, a condition glossary, a fair-price signal, seller ratings, and an off-platform fraud detector. But those primitives are **under-merchandised and self-reported**: there is no verification layer, no returns/guarantee mechanism anywhere in the codebase, and the fraud warning that's already written (`lib/safety.js` `SAFETY_WARNING`) is not shown in the one place it matters (the message composer). The highest-leverage work is not new features — it's **surfacing and hardening the trust the product already has.**

## ACQUISITION

### A1. Capture email demand on the homepage hero, not just on dead-end searches
- **Stage:** Acquisition → Activation
- **What & why:** `EmailAlertForm` (no account required) only appears on `app/browse/page.js` when a search returns **zero results**. That's backwards — you're capturing the most disappointed users and ignoring everyone who lands, browses, and leaves without buying (the ~87% who research online but buy offline). The homepage hero (`app/page.js`) has three secondary text links but no "get notified when tires near you are listed" capture. At launch, when inventory is thin, email demand-capture is the most valuable asset you can build. Add the alert form to the hero and to the `sizes`/`tires` SEO landing pages (which currently have no capture at all).
- **Where:** `app/page.js` hero; `app/sizes/[size]/page.js`; `app/tires/[brand]/page.js`; reuse `components/EmailAlertForm.js`.
- **Effort:** S · **Confidence:** High

### A2. Add incentive copy + trust to the email-alert form
- **Stage:** Acquisition
- **What & why:** `components/EmailAlertForm.js` has no incentive line, no "unsubscribe anytime," and no frequency disclosure. "Be first — get notified before matching sets sell" plus a one-line privacy reassurance measurably lifts opt-in. Low effort, rides on A1.
- **Where:** `components/EmailAlertForm.js`.
- **Effort:** S · **Confidence:** Med

### A3. Link the size SEO pages to the buying guide (parity with brand pages)
- **Stage:** Acquisition (SEO/dwell)
- **What & why:** `app/tires/[brand]/page.js` has a "Know before you buy" block linking to `/guide` plus 3 FAQs — a strong educational trust funnel. `app/sizes/[size]/page.js` has none. Size pages are your highest-intent SEO surface ("225/45R17 tires for sale"); adding the same guide link + FAQ improves dwell, internal linking, and trust with near-zero effort.
- **Where:** `app/sizes/[size]/page.js` (copy the pattern from `app/tires/[brand]/page.js`).
- **Effort:** S · **Confidence:** High

## ACTIVATION

### V1. The email-confirmation gate blocks first listings and first messages
- **Stage:** Activation (highest-leverage activation item)
- **What & why:** `AuthForm` signup returns a "check your email, link expires in 24 hours" modal before the account is usable, and there's no verified-email bypass in `lib/auth.js`. For a launch marketplace this is a hard activation cliff: a seller who wants to list right now, or a buyer who wants to message right now, is bounced to their inbox and many never return. Consider letting users **browse and start a listing draft immediately** and only requiring confirmation before the listing goes public / the first message sends — or at minimum add a prominent "resend" and make the confirmation email fast and obviously branded. This is a product decision, but it's the biggest single activation leak.
- **Where:** `components/AuthForm.js`, `lib/auth.js`, `app/signup/page.js`.
- **Effort:** M · **Confidence:** Med (impact High; the right fix needs Evan's call on how strict verification should be at launch)

### V2. Make "Message seller" work for logged-out buyers without a full detour
- **Stage:** Activation → Conversion
- **What & why:** On `app/listings/[id]/page.js`, `components/MessageSeller.js` shows logged-out users only "Log in to message." The primary conversion action on the primary conversion page requires an account first. Since the guided default message already exists ("Hi! Is this set still available?"), let a logged-out buyer **type their message first, then create an account on send** (message preserved through auth). Reduces the abandonment between intent and account creation.
- **Where:** `components/MessageSeller.js`, `app/api/threads/route.js`.
- **Effort:** M · **Confidence:** Med

### V3. Surface bulk-add and "fits your vehicle" where the user actually is
- **Stage:** Activation
- **What & why:** Two discoverability gaps: (1) The bulk-add tool is only reachable from `/pro` or a dashboard button — a Pro seller filling out the single-listing form in `app/sell/page.js` gets no "listing a batch? Use bulk add" nudge. (2) `HeroSearch` vehicle tab resolves a fitment size but after landing on `/browse`, there's no "confirm this fits your [vehicle]" reinforcement on the listing detail page even though `?fits=` is already threaded through browse. Small nudges, meaningful activation lift.
- **Where:** `app/sell/page.js` (bulk nudge); `app/listings/[id]/page.js` + `components/HeroSearch.js` (fitment reinforcement).
- **Effort:** S–M · **Confidence:** Med

## CONVERSION

### C1. Show the safety warning inside the message composer (it's already written)
- **Stage:** Conversion → Trust
- **What & why:** `lib/safety.js` exports `SAFETY_WARNING` and a working `detectOffPlatform()` detector, and it's shown on the **listing description** (`app/listings/[id]/page.js`). But it is **not shown in `components/MessageSeller.js`** — the exact moment a buyer is about to negotiate and is most exposed to "text me / pay by Zelle" scams. Rendering the existing warning (or triggering it on `detectOffPlatform` of the seller's reply) closes the top fraud vector on local marketplaces with code you already have. This is the cheapest trust win in the repo.
- **Where:** `components/MessageSeller.js` (import `SAFETY_WARNING` from `lib/safety.js`).
- **Effort:** S · **Confidence:** High

### C2. Introduce a returns / inspection-guarantee mechanism — the missing wedge
- **Stage:** Conversion → Trust (the strategic bet)
- **What & why:** There is **no returns, guarantee, or buyer-protection mechanism anywhere** in the codebase — the FAQ (`lib/content.js`) explicitly frames it as "arrange payment and pickup directly, just like a local marketplace… pay once you're satisfied." That "inspect in person" model is honest but it's exactly the incumbents' weakness, not a wedge. Ada's intel says the defensible position is a returns/guarantee + verified condition. Even a **lightweight, launch-appropriate version** — a standardized "inspect-before-you-pay" checklist enforced in the flow, a seller-attested "condition guarantee" badge, or a small in-person inspection window commitment — would differentiate. This is a business/product decision (it can't be shipped as pure code), but it's the highest strategic-leverage item in the memo and deserves a decision before launch. Monetization is fixed, so this is a trust feature, not a revenue lever.
- **Where:** New surface; touches `app/listings/[id]/page.js`, `lib/content.js` FAQ, seller onboarding.
- **Effort:** L · **Confidence:** Med (impact High)

### C3. Confirm the fair-price "deal" badge renders on the browse grid, and link its comps
- **Stage:** Conversion
- **What & why:** `lib/pricing.js` computes a strong "X% below the typical price" signal, and `app/browse/page.js` clearly computes `_fair` per card and passes it into `ListingCard`. Good — but verify it's visually prominent on the grid (the detail-page version in `app/listings/[id]/page.js` is well-styled; the card version should match). Also, the badge cites "avg $X/tire (N listings)" but there's no "see those N comps" link — making it clickable turns a claim into verifiable proof, which is what converts skeptical used-tire buyers.
- **Where:** `components/ListingCard.js`, `app/listings/[id]/page.js` (fair-price block).
- **Effort:** S · **Confidence:** Med

### C4. Add a "Message seller" CTA on the seller profile and a profile link on cards
- **Stage:** Conversion
- **What & why:** `app/sellers/[id]/page.js` shows ratings, reviews, sold count, member-since — good social proof — but has **no way to contact the seller from the profile** (you must go back into a listing). And `components/ListingCard.js` doesn't link to the seller profile, so the review social-proof is stranded. Cross-link them: profile → strongest listing / message; card → seller profile.
- **Where:** `app/sellers/[id]/page.js`, `components/ListingCard.js`.
- **Effort:** S · **Confidence:** Med

## RETENTION

### R1. The empty-inventory reality at launch needs a stronger seller-side pull
- **Stage:** Retention (supply side) → the launch cold-start
- **What & why:** Homepage (`app/page.js`) and browse (`app/browse/page.js`) both degrade gracefully to "No listings yet — be the first to sell" / email capture, which is correct. But a two-sided marketplace launches supply-first. The seller value prop in `app/sell-tires/page.js` and `lib/content.js` (`SELLER_BENEFITS`) is solid ("no Marketplace chaos," "first year free"). The retention risk is that **early sellers who list into an empty demand pool churn.** Consider seeding a "N buyers searching for your sizes this week" signal on the dashboard (`app/dashboard/page.js` already surfaces views/chats) to give early sellers a reason to stay before organic demand arrives.
- **Where:** `app/dashboard/page.js`.
- **Effort:** M · **Confidence:** Low (depends on real early traffic data)

## TRUST (cross-cutting, mostly folded above)

### T1. Add a seller response-time signal
- **Stage:** Trust → Conversion
- **What & why:** The listing detail seller card (`app/listings/[id]/page.js`) hard-codes "⚡ Usually responds within a day" **only for Pro sellers** — it's decorative, not measured. A real "avg reply: N hours" (computable from thread timestamps) on the seller profile and card is a credible urgency + trust signal and rewards responsive sellers regardless of tier.
- **Where:** `app/listings/[id]/page.js`, `app/sellers/[id]/page.js`.
- **Effort:** M · **Confidence:** Med

### T2. "Verified Pro sellers" is promised on the homepage but is just a paywall badge
- **Stage:** Trust
- **What & why:** The homepage TRUST bullet (`app/page.js`) says "Verified Pro sellers — Ratings, reviews, and trusted-seller badges you can vet." But `PRO` is purely a $25/mo subscription flag (`app/pro/page.js`) — there is no verification behind the word "verified." That's a credibility risk if a buyer gets burned by a "Verified Pro" scammer. Either soften the homepage copy ("Pro sellers") or attach a real (even light) verification step to the badge. Copy change is trivial; real verification is a product decision.
- **Where:** `app/page.js` (TRUST array), `lib/content.js`.
- **Effort:** S (copy) / L (real verification) · **Confidence:** Med

## Note on brand identity (flagging a discrepancy, not a recommendation)
The brief describes the intended identity as **brutalist — black + acid-yellow #e5ff00, Courier headers, Georgia body, square edges.** The **shipped app does not match this**: `app/page.js`, `app/sell-tires/page.js`, etc. use `font-display`, `rounded-3xl`/`rounded-2xl` corners, gradient fills, and slate/`brand`/`accent` tokens — a polished dark-SaaS aesthetic. The frozen `docs/index.html` matches the *live* app, not the brutalist brief. This isn't a funnel defect (the current look is clean and credible, arguably *better* for trust than raw brutalism), but flagging it because the design direction and the codebase have diverged — worth a conscious decision on which is the source of truth before launch marketing sets expectations.
